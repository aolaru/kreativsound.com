<p>Finishing a small sound pack is mostly a consistency job. The material can stay raw and characterful, but every file should arrive with the same practical expectations: clean starts and ends, sensible level, useful names, and a way to audition the collection.</p>

<h2>1. Start with a delivery profile</h2>
<p>Choose the profile closest to the destination before making individual decisions. Wave Mutator includes profiles for sample packs, music, game SFX, and store previews so cleanup, export, naming, pack, and preview defaults begin from a useful baseline.</p>

<h2>2. Review the source instead of flattening it</h2>
<p>Use the local cleanup controls to trim unwanted silence, add fades where a cut feels abrupt, and normalize peak level when the collection needs a more even starting point. Listen to material that has intentional tails or low-level detail before applying a broad treatment.</p>

<h2>3. Export the working deliverable</h2>
<p>Export cleaned WAV files when the pack needs to stay open for editing, or build a ZIP when the files are ready to hand over. The goal is not a final mix: it is a coherent set of raw materials that opens cleanly in the next session.</p>

<h2>4. Make a short listening path</h2>
<p>Use a local MP3 preview montage to give someone a fast sense of the pack's range. A concise sequence of contrasts—low texture, bright detail, movement, then a final transition—usually communicates more than a long unstructured reel.</p>

<p>Wave Mutator keeps this preparation in the browser, so source audio stays on the machine while the pack takes shape.</p>
