<p>Prepare a small batch of WAV files with consistent starts, ends, levels, and names, then export it with a short preview. Keep the original files so you can compare or revise the cleanup.</p>

<h2>1. Start with a delivery profile</h2>
<p>Choose the profile closest to the destination before making individual decisions. Wave Mutator Free includes profiles for sample packs, music, game SFX, and store previews so cleanup, export, naming, pack, and preview defaults begin from a useful baseline.</p>

<h2>2. Review the source instead of flattening it</h2>
<p>Preview the quietest file and the longest tail first. Trim unwanted silence, add a short fade at abrupt cuts, and normalize only when needed. Review warnings and compare with the original before applying the same settings to the batch.</p>

<h2>3. Export the working deliverable</h2>
<p>Export cleaned WAV files when the pack needs to stay open for editing, or build a ZIP when the files are ready to hand over. The goal is not a final mix: it is a coherent set of raw materials that opens cleanly in the next session.</p>

<h2>4. Make a short listening path</h2>
<p>Use a local MP3 preview montage to give someone a fast sense of the pack's range. A concise sequence of contrasts—low texture, bright detail, movement, then a final transition—usually communicates more than a long unstructured reel.</p>

<p>Wave Mutator Free keeps this preparation in the browser, so source audio stays on the machine while the pack takes shape.</p>
